# 1. Reference hierarchy

The target is **not an average of several aesthetics**. Each reference owns a different visual or interaction problem.

## A. Rutu Modan / *Exit Wounds* — people, social observation, comic grammar

Use for:

- adult bodies and faces that are specific rather than beautified;
- clear hand-drawn contour and flat local colour;
- ordinary clothing and posture;
- urban/social realism without photographic stiffness;
- establishing-frame → medium/close/insert comic sequencing;
- dry, unsentimental humour and interpersonal awkwardness;
- environments that explain class, work and social position.

Do not request an exact imitation of Rutu Modan. The production target is the high-level grammar recorded in `ART_DIRECTION.md`.

Official/public references:
- Drawn & Quarterly, *Exit Wounds*: https://drawnandquarterly.com/books/exit-wounds/
- The Comics Journal interview: https://www.tcj.com/rutu-modan/2/

## B. *Death and Taxes* — tactile interaction and diegetic interface

Use for:

- the desk/work surface as a persistent playable stage;
- papers, files, tools and devices as manipulable objects instead of abstract tabs;
- restrained 2D theatre-like spaces;
- decisions embedded in handling objects/documents;
- institutional props such as forms, stamps, folders, receipts, drawers and telephones;
- a small interaction vocabulary reused across many cases.

Do not copy its black/grey/yellow identity. Borrow the **physicality and stage grammar**, not the UI skin.

Official/public references:
- Presskit: https://deathandtaxesgame.com/presskit
- Steam: https://store.steampowered.com/app/1166290/Death_and_Taxes/

## C. soupsoup — scene density, looking as play, micro-stories

Use for:

- illustration-first scenes with very little conventional UI;
- many small objects and side-events that reward looking;
- several object/figure scales in one composition;
- strong silhouette separation despite high density;
- hidden-object discovery inside ordinary visual richness;
- strange, playful spatial composition rather than strict photographic perspective;
- touch-friendly direct interaction with the illustrated world.

Do not copy its cute tone, mascot proportions or target-strip UI. Sunflower is older, stranger, drier, more socially grounded and less toy-like.

Official/public references:
- Steam: https://store.steampowered.com/app/4201610/soupsoup/
- App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/soupsoup-magazine/id6443660171
- Google Play: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.madkang.hotcornsoup
- hotcornsoup links: https://linktr.ee/hotcornsoup

## D. Abbas Kiarostami, *The Colours* / *Rang-ha* (1976) — colour as a way of noticing and organising ordinary objects

This is now a **resolved major reference**, not an unresolved placeholder.

The film is a short picture-book/formal exercise built around colours appearing across everyday objects, nature and urban life. Its value to Sunflower is not children's-film tone; it is the way a simple visual code makes the viewer **notice relations among otherwise ordinary things**.

Use for:

- colour attached to concrete objects/surfaces rather than atmospheric grading;
- visual rhymes between unrelated objects sharing a hue;
- moving attention from colour → object → context → another object;
- ordinary consumer/work objects becoming graphic subjects;
- a limited frame making one mundane thing highly legible;
- rhythmic sequences of object inserts;
- colour as an indexing/memory device rather than a rarity/status system;
- playful formal association without explanatory prose.

Sunflower translation:

> Colour should help the player *see the harbour's material relationships* before the UI names them.

Examples of the grammar, not frozen palette assignments:

- a rust-red crate, bar receipt stamp and repaired chair can rhyme visually without meaning the same mechanic;
- a turquoise glass, wet harbour paint and notebook mark can recur across separate scenes and create memory;
- a strong yellow object can become the visual hinge of a panel without receiving a quest glow;
- repeated goods can be recognised by colour/form before reading their labels;
- a consequence montage can move through several ordinary objects linked by one colour instead of explaining the connection in text.

Do **not** turn this into:

- colour-coded loot rarity;
- red = bad / green = good morality coding;
- each NPC owning one superhero colour;
- flat monochrome scene filters;
- toddler pedagogy;
- literal imitation of the film's object sequences.

Public/official references:
- Janus Films: https://www.janusfilms.com/films/1929
- Criterion: https://www.criterion.com/films/29520-colors
- MK2 Films: https://mk2films.com/en/film/the-colours/
- Asian Film Archive: https://asianfilmarchive.org/event-calendar/short-films-1-retrospective-abbas-kiarostami/

## E. *The Colour of Pomegranates* — tableau and material choreography

Use for:

- frontal or nearly frontal tableaux;
- shallow/flattened spatial depth;
- people and objects arranged as living still life;
- fruit, books, vessels, cloth, metal, flowers, liquids and tools functioning compositionally;
- stillness interrupted by one small physical act;
- recurring objects whose meaning changes with context;
- saturated material colour with strong object isolation.

Do not copy religious images, costumes or exact tableaux. Sunflower should not become mystical by default.

Public reference:
- BFI: https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/where-begin-with-sergei-parajanov

## F. *Chess of the Wind* — architecture as social pressure

Use for:

- controlled architectural framing;
- doors, arches, screens, furniture and distance dividing people;
- deep jewel/dark earth colours against pale paper/metal/skin;
- formal distance versus conspiratorial proximity;
- money, inheritance, provenance and objects turning a beautiful room into a pressure chamber;
- still, deliberate blocking rather than cinematic coverage.

Strongest translation:
- Yasmin's viewing room / Auction;
- Dima's private settlement room;
- high-stakes finance/provenance scenes.

Public references:
- BFI / Sight and Sound: https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/rediscovery-chess-wind
- Janus Films: https://www.janusfilms.com/films/2017

## G. *Azur & Asmar* — ornamental clarity and graphic architecture

Use for:

- ultramarine/cobalt, turquoise, green, gold/ochre, terracotta and cream relationships;
- strong silhouette separation;
- arches, lattices, geometric pattern and textile rhythm;
- large clean colour areas carrying detailed ornament;
- architecture that can be visually rich without photoreal depth.

Do not borrow:
- children's-animation body proportions;
- fairy-tale princess framing;
- decorative overload;
- generic "Arabian Nights" shorthand.

Official reference:
- Michel Ocelot: https://www.michelocelot.com/azur-asmar

## H. Día de Muertos material culture — meaningful accumulation, humour beside mortality

Use for:

- tables/altars as dense but legible accumulations of objects;
- flower, food, paper, photograph, candle, vessel and personal object sharing one field;
- marigold orange and purple/black/white/pink/green as examples of strong printed contrast;
- cut-paper layering and repeated motif;
- death imagery coexisting with humour, care, appetite and ordinary life.

Do not turn Sunflower into a Día de Muertos theme or use sugar skulls as generic decoration.

Reference:
- Wereldmuseum Amsterdam: https://amsterdam.wereldmuseum.nl/en/collection-stories/dia-de-muertos

## I. Hidden Folks — engineering precedent, not primary style

Use for:

- large hand-built scenes made from layered elements;
- many unique micro-interactions in one illustration;
- mouse/keyboard/touch compatibility;
- hidden discovery without score/time pressure;
- draw/place/layer/script production logic.

Official/public reference:
- Press kit: https://hiddenfolks.com/press

# 2. What the creator-supplied references imply for Sunflower

## 2.1 The scene must contain more life than the current task

Every major WIDE scene needs three simultaneous layers:

1. **mechanically active** people/objects;
2. **informative traces** that reveal work, prices, relationships, weather, obligations or earlier actions;
3. **non-utility visual life** that may never become useful.

If the player has learned every current action and there is nothing left worth looking at, the scene is under-authored.

## 2.2 Objects carry narrative before faces do

Prefer a meaningful prop/state trace over another facial-expression sprite.

Examples:

- untouched second drink;
- damp route paper;
- rewrapped parcel;
- clipped provenance photograph;
- bruised lime visible through a crate opening;
- worn collateral tag;
- hand-written bar experiment;
- plant tied around an old label;
- returned Octopus tray;
- hidden toad behind an object whose presence already makes sense.

## 2.3 Colour should produce recognition, association and rhythm

This is the specific contribution of *The Colours*.

A scene palette is not merely a mood palette. It should create a **visual syntax**:

- one or two dominant fields establish the place;
- a few recurring object colours guide the eye;
- related colours can rhyme across WIDE → WORK → consequence INSERT;
- a later scene may repeat a colour/object relationship to trigger recognition;
- flat colour should make ordinary goods readable without icon badges;
- colour may carry ambiguity and coincidence; it must not promise a mechanic that does not exist.

The renderer/art manifest should therefore allow per-scene/per-prop colour variants where authored, without baking semantic gameplay meaning into a colour constant.

## 2.4 Stillness matters

Pomegranates/Chess/Modan all support a world where a held posture, an object placement, distance between two people or an empty chair can carry meaning.

Do not add motion merely to prove the scene is alive. Small loops should be exceptional enough that the Sun Moment's stopping of ordinary motion remains perceptible.

# 3. Explicit negatives

Sunflower must **not** drift toward:

- pixel art;
- chibi/cute mascot game;
- kawaii/cozy framing as the default tone;
- anime/webtoon beauty rendering;
- glossy vector startup illustration;
- 3D mobile-game character splash art;
- visual-novel portrait boxes over a background;
- Twine/ChoiceScript-like text-adventure panels;
- card-dashboard UI where places/people are represented mainly by rectangular cards;
- photoreal/cinematic concept art;
- generic "indie hand-drawn" without materially specific environments;
- a Where's-Waldo clone where scenes exist only for hidden-object hunting;
- commercial hospitality moodboarding as a visual source of truth;
- theme-restaurant shorthand for Arabic/North-African influence.

Real venues may help answer factual spatial questions, but they are **not visual canon**. Joel's Bar must be authored from Sunflower's world, economy, characters and the art/cinema/comic references above.
