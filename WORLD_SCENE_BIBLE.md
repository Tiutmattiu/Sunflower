# 1. The harbour is an illustrated situation, not a decorated interface

A location should feel like the player entered a **carefully composed, materially specific situation that existed before them**.

Every major scene must support at once:

- **economic function** — what work, exchange, risk or care happens here;
- **social function** — who waits, meets, avoids, owes, watches or performs here;
- **visual pleasure** — the scene remains worth looking at when no action is immediately useful;
- **direct interaction** — people and objects are physical scene elements rather than menu cards;
- **comic composition** — the tableau can naturally become WIDE / MEDIUM / CLOSE / INSERT panels;
- **world residue** — earlier actions leave paper, mess, absence, rearranged furniture, goods or other traces.

Target feeling:

> an interactive printed comic / illustrated magazine world containing a living economy.

Not:

> a sophisticated web dashboard with drawings attached.

# 2. Visual references have separate jobs

The creator's primary reference set is now:

- **Rutu Modan / Exit Wounds** — adult people, social observation, ordinary bodies, comic grammar;
- **Death and Taxes** — tactile work surface, institutional objects, diegetic interaction;
- **soupsoup** — scene density, visual exploration, micro-stories, hidden discovery;
- **Abbas Kiarostami, The Colours / Rang-ha** — colour as a way to notice and associate ordinary objects;
- **The Colour of Pomegranates** — flattened tableau and material choreography;
- **Chess of the Wind** — enclosed architecture, stillness, social pressure around property and power;
- **Azur & Asmar** — graphic architecture, ornament and saturated colour clarity;
- **Día de Muertos material culture** — dense meaningful accumulation, humour/care beside mortality;
- **Hidden Folks** — engineering precedent for layered interactive illustration.

These are not a blended moodboard. Each owns a production problem.

## 2.1 The Colours — colour as material indexing

This is now a resolved major reference.

The useful lesson is not children's-film tone. It is the way ordinary objects become newly visible when the viewer is asked to notice a colour and then discover that colour across nature, consumer objects and urban life.

Use this in Sunflower to make colour do cognitive work:

- attach colour to real objects/surfaces, not full-screen grading;
- create visual rhymes among unrelated objects;
- move the eye colour → object → context → another object;
- let repeated object colours build memory across locations;
- use a strong colour object as a panel hinge without giving it a quest glow;
- allow WIDE → WORK → INSERT sequences to retain one colour relationship;
- let ordinary goods become graphic subjects before text labels explain them.

Do not turn colour into:

- loot rarity;
- morality coding;
- fixed NPC superhero colours;
- a traffic-light UI language for every mechanic;
- scene-wide monochrome filters.

Colour can help the player remember; it must not falsely claim a rule.

## 2.2 The Colour of Pomegranates — living still life

Use:

- frontal or nearly frontal arrangements;
- shallow/flattened depth;
- people and objects composed together as graphic masses;
- textiles, books, fruit, vessels, metal, flowers, tools, liquids and paper as visual actors;
- stillness interrupted by one small physical action;
- repeated objects changing meaning by context.

Translation:

> goods, claims, invitations, plants, tools and people can share one tableau without hierarchy being expressed as UI chrome.

Do not copy specific sacred imagery, costumes or film tableaux.

## 2.3 Chess of the Wind — architecture as pressure

Use:

- doors, arches, screens, furniture and negative space to divide people;
- formal distance vs conspiratorial proximity;
- deep jewel/dark earth colour against paper/metal/skin;
- still blocking around money, ownership, inheritance, provenance and obligation;
- rooms that are beautiful but socially dangerous.

Strongest use:

- Yasmin's viewing room / Auction;
- Dima's settlement room;
- rare high-stakes finance/provenance scenes.

Do not turn the whole harbour into a palace or period reconstruction.

## 2.4 Azur & Asmar — graphic architecture without photoreal burden

Use:

- ultramarine/cobalt, turquoise, green, gold/ochre, terracotta and cream relationships;
- strong silhouette separation;
- arches, lattices, screens, geometric pattern and textile rhythm;
- large clean colour areas that can carry detailed ornament;
- architecture that feels designed without realistic depth rendering.

Do not borrow:

- children's-animation proportions;
- fairy-tale princess tone;
- ornamental overload;
- generic Arabian-Nights shorthand.

## 2.5 Día de Muertos — meaningful accumulation

Use selectively for:

- tables/shelves as legible accumulations of objects;
- food, flowers, paper, photograph, candle, vessel and personal object sharing a field;
- cut-paper/layered graphic rhythm;
- strong printed colour contrast;
- mortality coexisting with humour, care, appetite and everyday life.

Do not turn the harbour into a Día de Muertos theme or use decorative skulls as a shortcut.

# 3. Joel's Bar — flagship social world

Joel's Bar is a major economic and social convergence scene. It needs more authorship than a generic tavern.

It is **not based on a Hong Kong venue** and should not use real commercial hospitality interiors as visual canon.

Its design must emerge from:

1. Joel's actual operating economy and Night Menu;
2. the people who repeatedly use the room;
3. the creator's desire for a chic, artistic Arabic/North-African shisha-lounge atmosphere;
4. the comic/cinema grammar above;
5. accumulated harbour history.

The bar should feel **cosmopolitan, intimate, strange, worked-in and personally assembled**, not professionally themed.

## 3.1 Ingredient craft is world logic, not venue imitation

Joel's economy already justifies visible:

- citrus;
- herbs;
- spices;
- syrups;
- cacao or dark aromatic ingredients where appropriate;
- jars/infusions;
- ice;
- glassware;
- strainer/jigger/spoon/press;
- handwritten trials;
- half-prepared stock;
- spoiled/leftover material;
- invoices, tabs and delivery notes.

The player should understand that Joel **makes things** before any dialogue says so.

This comes from the runtime menu and bar operation, not from copying an outside cocktail bar.

## 3.2 Arabic / North-African shisha-lounge atmosphere

Treat this as **atmosphere and material vocabulary**, not historical/geographic reconstruction.

Useful elements:

- limewash/plaster walls;
- restrained arches or niches;
- carved/perforated screens or lattice in limited areas;
- low or mixed-height seating pockets;
- dark wood;
- woven textiles;
- one or two geometric tile/pattern zones rather than every surface;
- brass/copper details used sparsely;
- glass, water, metal and smoke as small reflective/moving accents;
- warm practical lighting without nightclub gloss;
- terracotta/cacao/rust against cobalt/turquoise/bottle-green moments;
- shisha/hookah as a real social object only when somebody is actually using or handling it.

Avoid:

- lantern-wall clichés;
- fake Arabic calligraphy used as wallpaper;
- gold-on-black luxury branding;
- desert silhouettes;
- belly-dancer imagery;
- permanent hookah iconography;
- generic palace arches on every wall;
- a purchased "Middle Eastern theme" look.

The room should feel as if Joel slowly accumulated useful and loved objects, not as if a hospitality design company installed a concept.

## 3.3 WIDE composition

The WIDE should be dense enough to explore but organized by a few strong masses:

1. **bar/work counter** — horizontal work mass;
2. **window / harbour edge** — secondary air/light mass;
3. **low seating pocket** — intimate social cluster;
4. **back storage / niches** — ingredient + paper + object field;
5. **door / circulation edge** — people arriving, waiting or leaving.

Do not use a perfectly symmetrical catalogue shot.

Allow:

- an oversized foreground person or prop;
- compressed depth;
- a partial cutaway wall;
- tiny background social activity;
- one strong colour object that organizes the eye;
- incidental clutter that is not clickable.

## 3.4 WORK composition

Focus on:

- Joel's hands/tools;
- one current ingredient/object;
- a tab/claim/order/invitation;
- one customer/counterparty;
- one secondary trace such as watch, parcel, unfinished drink, receipt or route note.

A WORK scene should already read as a comic panel before actions are overlaid.

## 3.6 Persistent clutter

Examples:

- citrus peel bowl;
- spice/cacao jars;
- uneven bottle group;
- hand press / strainer / jigger / spoon;
- menu drafts;
- tab book;
- receipt spike or clipped receipts;
- chipped tray;
- cloth/towel;
- water jug;
- one personally accumulated art object;
- small plant;
- borrowed chair;
- repaired stool;
- wet umbrella/coat after weather;
- shisha only when socially relevant.

Not all clutter is actionable.

## 3.7 State through objects, not labels

Examples:

- Aspen leaving soon → body angled to exit + watch/route note + unfinished drink;
- Juan under tab pressure → tab book physically nearer him, less casual serving clutter;
- Yasmin hosting → catalogue/envelope + reserved chair + controlled empty space;
- Dima settlement → sealed envelope + claim paper taking over a table;
- Wong handoff → parcel under/beside seat + string/paper residue;
- missed meeting → untouched second glass / folded note / empty chair;
- public sanction gossip → folded Octopus notice in the room, not automatically readable;
- toad-circle aftermath → damp container trace + strange phone notification + altered seating.

# 4. Location identities

Two locations should not be interchangeable after labels are removed.

## 4.1 Workbench / player's desk

Function:
- cognitive centre.

Visual language:
- handled paper, harbour grime, wood/metal surface;
- phone, notebook, ledger, newspaper, receipts and slips physically overlap;
- objects accumulate through play;
- Death and Taxes physicality + Pomegranates still-life logic + Kiarostami object clarity.

## 4.2 Harbour berth / Aspen

Function:
- movement, cargo, deadlines, route choice.

Visual language:
- rope, wet wood/metal, manifests, crates, boat edges, layered horizon, weather;
- cool harbour mass with rust/terracotta/cargo colour anchors;
- crews and small boats form background micro-stories;
- not a clean marina.

## 4.3 Wong's counter / storage

Function:
- cheap custody, packing, handoff, salvage, small service.

Visual language:
- dense but practically sorted;
- reused boxes, labels, string, bottle crates, chargers, locks, tags, paper;
- narrow paths and reachable shelves;
- floor/low anchors matter if Wong retains current Dog presentation;
- no whimsical junk-shop rainbow.

## 4.4 Juan's nursery

Function:
- biological productive capital, curiosity and risk.

Visual language:
- humid greenhouse/yard;
- tables at different heights;
- containers, labels, roots, cuttings, tools;
- ordinary plastic/wire/old wood/glass mixed with living colour;
- plant mass creates natural hidden-toad zones;
- handwritten measurement culture.

## 4.5 Yasmin's viewing room / Auction

Function:
- provenance, capital, access, social ranking, uncertainty.

Visual language:
- controlled chamber architecture;
- arches/screens/divisions without exact period copying;
- deep green/wine/ink/cream with brass/silver accents;
- lots isolated on cloth/pedestal/table rather than retail shelves;
- people separated by furniture/negative space;
- Chess of the Wind pressure + Modan bodies + Pomegranates object choreography;
- strong single-colour objects may guide the eye Kiarostami-style.

## 4.6 Dima's back room

Function:
- private settlement, claims, proxy, enforcement, black-market access.

Visual language:
- small, quiet, procedural;
- envelopes, keys, tags, numbered papers, old calculator/ledger, bag/box;
- privacy without gangster cliché;
- current Seagull presentation needs believable perch/desk/railing anchors;
- architecture should make confidential proximity visible.

## 4.7 Octopus Clearing

Function:
- civic institution, public commitment and settlement.

Visual language:
- standardized trays, tags, receipts, slips, owner labels, queues/slots and returned piles;
- geometric repeatable structure;
- institutional colour discipline distinct from private merchants;
- no personified Octopus mascot desk;
- client goods remain visibly client-owned until settlement;
- public tape and release/return are physically legible.

Use Death and Taxes for handled institutional objects and Pomegranates for formal object arrangement.

## 4.8 Sonya's kitchen

Function:
- household hospitality and non-market allocation.

Visual language:
- small, worn, warm, practical;
- borrowed chairs;
- rare fresh fish preparation;
- serving dishes, old cloth, ordinary storage, household Sunflower;
- food and household objects may create a dense still-life table;
- invitation payoff is intimate, never a reward-screen spectacle.

## 4.9 Cliff path

Function:
- risk, mobility, distance from commerce.

Visual language:
- large negative space;
- simplified rock/sea/sky masses;
- tiny body/object scale against landscape;
- wind and posture matter more than UI;
- one isolated colour object can become striking precisely because the scene is sparse.

# 5. Colour system — from palette to visual syntax

Do not impose one global palette.

Recurring material families may include:

- cacao / burnt umber;
- terracotta / rust;
- marigold / old yellow;
- ultramarine / cobalt;
- turquoise / oxidised teal;
- bottle green / olive;
- dusty rose / pomegranate red;
- aubergine / muted purple;
- warm plaster / old paper;
- charcoal / ink;
- small brass/copper accents.

But the important rule from *The Colours* is:

> **colour belongs to things.**

For each scene, author:

1. 1–2 large dominant colour fields;
2. 2–4 recurring object colours that guide attention;
3. one or more visual rhymes with props/traces;
4. optional consequence-panel continuation of those colours.

Examples are compositional, not gameplay coding:

- a rust crate may rhyme with a chair repair and receipt stamp;
- cobalt glass may echo harbour paint or an auction cloth;
- marigold may isolate a flower, paper edge or fruit without making it a quest target.

High saturation is allowed.
It should feel matte/printed, not LED/neon/glossy.

The scene manifest may support authored colour variants, but gameplay truth must never depend on a player inferring an unfrozen colour code.

---

# 6. Pattern and ornament

Pattern is important but spatially controlled.

Good uses:

- one tiled strip;
- one woven textile field;
- paper cut edge;
- lattice/screen;
- printed label family;
- auction cloth;
- plant-shadow pattern;
- crate/grid geometry;
- niche repetition.

Bad use:

> every surface has an ornate pattern.

Dense scenes require large quiet fields.

---

# 7. Micro-story density

Each WIDE should contain things worth noticing when no route action is currently available.

Examples:

- chair moved to avoid somebody;
- parcel rewrapped;
- half-cleaned spill;
- background visitor counting coins;
- gull watching food;
- customer sleeping or reading;
- second glass implying someone expected;
- upside-down pinned note;
- plant grown around an old tag;
- Octopus tray containing returned goods;
- hidden toad integrated into ordinary clutter.

Do not annotate these by default.

Some may later become useful; others remain texture forever.

---

# 8. Motion restraint

Sunflower should not constantly wiggle.

Possible small loops:

- smoke curl;
- hanging cloth edge;
- water drip;
- fan/plant movement;
- condensation;
- tapping hand;
- lifting paper edge;
- distant boat;
- rare head/eye turn;
- toad micro-movement only when accessibility/authored conditions permit.

Stillness is part of the visual grammar.

The Sun Moment only works if stopping ordinary motion is perceptible.

# 9. Audio direction — deferred

Future audio may move toward:

- instrumental North-African / West-Asian / Mediterranean fusion;
- jazz / neo-soul / R&B harmonic/rhythmic language;
- occasional gnawa-informed pulse;
- oud, qanun, ney, guembri, riq/bendir or related timbres where musically coherent;
- Rhodes/electric piano, upright/electric bass, brushed drums, restrained horns/guitar;
- warm, groove-based, nocturnal, urban texture.

Avoid:

- tourist-restaurant "Arabian" loops;
- generic belly-dance patterns;
- EDM oriental presets;
- faux-mystical desert flute;
- constant dramatic oud;
- vocals that compete with dialogue by default.

Do not source BGM in the current production pass unless higher-priority world/interaction work is finished.

Any future track must have explicit repository licence provenance.

# 11. Acceptance for the production scene-authoring pass

Using placeholders where final art is absent, the implementation should demonstrate:

- Joel's Bar is a coherent illustrated social/work environment, not a dashboard;
- no real-world Hong Kong bar is used as the visual template;
- Arabic/North-African shisha-lounge atmosphere comes from material, seating, geometry, colour and social use rather than theme-restaurant clichés;
- actual Night Menu / Bar economy is visible through ingredients/tools/papers;
- The Colours' object/colour association principle is visible in at least several authored scene/panel compositions without becoming UI status coding;
- the same Bar WIDE supports multiple autonomous named-NPC combinations;
- active + trace + non-utility layers coexist;
- Yasmin, Wong, Juan, Dima, Octopus, Sonya and the berth are visually distinct before labels;
- consequence panels reuse scene assets and colour/object relationships;
- mobile reflow preserves scene reading;
- hidden toads can be genuinely camouflaged by world density;
- future final art is replaceable through manifest/Scene Lab rather than gameplay rewrite.

This is a world-building specification, not a moodboard.
