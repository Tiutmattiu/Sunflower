# 1. The world should feel staged, inhabited and materially specific

Sunflower is not a web page with illustrated cards.

A location should feel like the player walked into a **carefully composed illustrated situation** that existed before them.

Each scene must support all of the following at once:

- **economic function** — what work, exchange, care or risk happens here;
- **social function** — who waits, meets, avoids, owes, watches or performs here;
- **visual pleasure** — the player can look around without immediately needing a task;
- **direct interaction** — people and objects are physical hotspots rather than menu entries;
- **comic composition** — the tableau can naturally become later WIDE / MEDIUM / CLOSE / INSERT panels;
- **world residue** — earlier actions leave objects, mess, paper, absence, rearranged furniture or other traces.

The final feeling should be closer to an **interactive comic tableau / illustrated magazine spread** than a visual novel, RPG menu, card UI, pixel adventure or cute hidden-object game.

# 2. Expanded reference hierarchy

The creator's reference set now includes:

- Rutu Modan / *Exit Wounds* — social observation, adult bodies, comic sequencing;
- *Death and Taxes* — tactile desk/theatre interaction;
- soupsoup — dense illustration-first worlds and micro-stories;
- *The Colour of Pomegranates* — ritual tableau, flattened spatial composition, material symbolism;
- *Chess of the Wind* — dark chamber blocking, Persian interior geometry, social pressure, painterly stillness;
- *Azur & Asmar* — saturated North-African / Middle-Eastern ornamental geometry, arches, pattern and colour clarity;
- Día de Muertos material culture — accumulated objects, marigold colour, cut paper, celebration/death coexistence;
- Brisa HK — ingredient-led day/night bar identity and visible cocktail craft;
- contemporary Middle-Eastern / North-African lounge references such as ZZURA — stripped geometric arches, plaster, warm earth, cyan/orange accents, intimate pockets, souk-like spatial organisation.

These references do **not** have equal jobs.

## 2.1 The Colour of Pomegranates — tableau and material choreography

Use:

- frontal or nearly frontal arrangements;
- shallow, deliberately flattened depth;
- bodies arranged as graphic masses rather than cinematic coverage;
- recurring objects with symbolic/material weight;
- textiles, books, fruit, metal, vessels, flowers, tools, liquids and paper as compositional actors;
- stillness broken by a small physical action;
- saturated colour fields with strong object isolation;
- repeated motifs that change meaning through context.

Translate into Sunflower as:

> people + goods + documents + vessels + plants + tools can be arranged like a living still life.

Do **not** copy specific religious images, costumes, frames or tableaux from the film.
Do not make the harbour mystical by default.

Public reference:
- BFI Parajanov guide: https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/where-begin-with-sergei-parajanov

## 2.2 Chess of the Wind — enclosed social pressure

Use:

- controlled, architectural framing;
- rooms that divide people through doors, arches, screens, columns and furniture;
- candle/low practical light as a compositional source rather than generic cinematic glow;
- deep jewel/dark earth colour against pale faces/paper/metal;
- formal distance and conspiratorial proximity;
- still, almost ceremonial blocking around money, inheritance, objects and power;
- rooms that feel beautiful but socially dangerous.

Translate most strongly into:

- Yasmin's viewing room / auction;
- Dima's private settlement room;
- rare formal social scenes;
- moments when finance/provenance turns a room into a pressure chamber.

Do not turn every location into a palace or period interior.

Public references:
- BFI / Sight and Sound: https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/rediscovery-chess-wind
- Janus Films: https://www.janusfilms.com/films/2017

## 2.3 Azur & Asmar — ornamental clarity without realism burden

Use:

- strong silhouette separation;
- vivid ultramarine/cobalt, turquoise, green, gold/ochre, orange/terracotta and cream;
- arches, lattices, geometric pattern, carved openings and textile rhythm;
- vegetation and built form treated as graphic pattern;
- large clean colour areas holding highly detailed ornament;
- architecture that can be beautiful without photoreal depth.

Translate into Sunflower as a **design vocabulary**, not fairy-tale tone.

Avoid:

- childlike character proportions;
- fantasy-princess staging;
- decorative overload on every wall;
- generic "Arabian Nights" iconography.

Official reference:
- Michel Ocelot: https://www.michelocelot.com/azur-asmar

## 2.4 Día de Muertos — life, death and material accumulation

Use:

- marigold orange as a strong but limited accent;
- purple / black / white / pink / green in deliberate printed contrast;
- cut-paper / papel-picado logic as flat layered pattern;
- altars/tables as meaningful object accumulation;
- food, flowers, photos, candles, vessels and paper occupying the same visual field;
- death imagery coexisting with humour, care, food and ordinary social life.

Translate carefully into Sunflower's broader material world.
Do **not** make every scene a Día de Muertos theme or cover the harbour in sugar skulls.

Reference on papel picado / colour materiality:
- Wereldmuseum Amsterdam: https://amsterdam.wereldmuseum.nl/en/collection-stories/dia-de-muertos

## 2.5 Creator reference: “The Colours”

The creator named **“the colours”** as an important reference, but the exact work/title is not yet resolved in the repository.

Do not guess or freeze a substitute.
Keep a placeholder reference entry until the creator identifies the exact film/artwork.

# 3. Joel's Bar — Brisa function, less commercial, Arabic/North-African art-lounge mood

Joel's Bar is a major social/economic convergence scene and should receive more environmental authorship than a generic tavern.

## 3.1 What to take from Brisa HK

Brisa's public identity is useful because it is an **ingredient-driven space** rather than a generic alcohol shelf:

- cacao café by day / cocktail bar by night;
- visible transformation of function across the day;
- ingredient-led drinks;
- fresh produce, herbs, spices, house infusions and custom preparation;
- cocktail creation as craft/performance rather than merely menu selection;
- warm, vivid orange/cacao identity.

Public references:
- Epicurean Group: https://www.epicurean.com.hk/restaurant/brisa/
- Lee Gardens: https://www.leegardens.com.hk/dining/LG/8831/Brisa?lang=zh-HK

Sunflower translation:

- visible jars, infusions, citrus, herbs, syrups, cacao/spice containers, ice, tools and handwritten experiments;
- Joel's craft should be readable before dialogue;
- the room may shift subtly between daytime preparation and evening social convergence;
- unusual ingredient combinations are part of the room's visual identity.

## 3.2 What NOT to take from Brisa

Sunflower should **not** look like a polished luxury-mall F&B concept.

Reduce:

- corporate neatness;
- perfect symmetry;
- empty marble surfaces;
- showroom backlighting;
- overly branded displays;
- Instagram-first cleanliness;
- generic "premium cocktail lab" language.

Replace with:

- smaller scale;
- visible wear;
- irregular storage;
- personal experiments;
- repaired furniture;
- borrowed chairs;
- handwritten notes;
- old bottles and new ingredients coexisting;
- evidence of tabs, deliveries, arguments, meetings and rushed closing;
- artistic objects that look accumulated rather than purchased as a design package.

## 3.3 Contemporary Middle-Eastern / North-African lounge layer

The desired bar mood is **chic, intimate, artistic, North-African / Arabic-adjacent**, but not a theme restaurant.

Useful material/spatial vocabulary:

- plaster / limewash walls;
- simple arches and niches;
- low or mixed-height seating pockets;
- dark wood;
- brass/copper used selectively;
- woven textiles;
- flat geometric tile/zellige-inspired pattern in limited zones;
- screens/lattices used to create partial privacy;
- low amber practical lights;
- occasional cyan/teal/green contrast against terracotta/cacao/orange;
- water vessel / basin / glass / metal reflections as small accents;
- shisha equipment as a real social object when present, not a permanent logo;
- smoke only as occasional environmental depth, never a constant fog filter.

A particularly useful contemporary precedent is ZZURA in Hong Kong: stripped Middle-Eastern geometry, souk-like pockets, warm plaster and a restrained cyan/orange palette rather than maximal historical pastiche.

References:
- Design Anthology: https://design-anthology.com/story/interiors-hong-kong-speakeasy-zurra-bar
- ZZURA: https://www.zzura.hk/

Bushra is a secondary reference for the *idea* of a space switching between restaurant/lounge/shisha uses, but Sunflower should avoid its more overt nightclub/performance/"exotic" staging.

Reference:
- MAG Studio: https://www.mag-studios.com/portfolio2/bushra

## 3.4 Anti-orientalist rule

Do not solve "Arabic/North-African" by piling up:

- lantern clichés;
- belly-dancer imagery;
- fake palace arches everywhere;
- hookahs as wallpaper;
- gold-on-black luxury branding;
- generic desert silhouettes;
- random script as decoration;
- "mystical East" fantasy.

Use **materials, geometry, social seating, object handling, colour and craft**.

The room should feel like Joel collected references and objects because he likes them, not like a hospitality firm purchased a theme package.

# 4. Joel's Bar composition bible

## 4.1 WIDE view

The WIDE should feel dense enough to explore but structurally clear.

Suggested masses:

1. **bar/work counter** — one strong horizontal mass;
2. **window / outer harbour edge** — secondary light/air mass;
3. **seating pocket** — low intimate cluster;
4. **back storage / niches** — ingredient/object wall;
5. **door / circulation edge** — people may arrive, wait or leave.

Avoid a perfectly frontal symmetrical cocktail-bar catalogue shot.

Allow one foreground object/person to crop aggressively into frame, Modan/soupsoup-style, when socially useful.

## 4.2 WORK view

Focus on:

- Joel's hands/tools;
- current ingredient/object;
- tab/claim/order paper;
- one customer/counterparty;
- secondary evidence such as watch, parcel, unfinished glass, receipt, route note.

The WORK view should feel like a comic panel that happens to be interactive.

## 4.3 Anchor families

Recommended semantic anchors:

- `bar_inside_left`
- `bar_inside_right`
- `counter_customer_left`
- `counter_customer_right`
- `window_seat_a`
- `window_seat_b`
- `low_lounge_a`
- `low_lounge_b`
- `back_table_a`
- `back_table_b`
- `door_wait`
- `door_exit`
- `floor_wong`
- `perch_dima`
- `counter_prop_a`
- `counter_prop_b`
- `table_prop_a`
- `table_prop_b`
- `hidden_toad_zone_1`
- `hidden_toad_zone_2`

Scene Lab should allow authors to move these without touching gameplay code.

## 4.4 Persistent clutter

Not all clutter is actionable.

Persistent or semi-persistent examples:

- citrus peel bowl;
- spice/cacao jars;
- uneven bottles;
- hand press / strainer / jigger / spoon;
- old menu drafts;
- matchbox / coaster / receipt;
- chipped tray;
- cloth/towel;
- water jug;
- one framed or pinned art object;
- small plant;
- hookah/shisha equipment only when socially relevant;
- borrowed chair;
- repaired stool;
- wet umbrella / coat after weather.

## 4.5 State traces

Show state through objects rather than labels:

- Aspen leaving soon → watch/route note, body turned toward door, drink unfinished;
- Juan tab pressure → tab book pushed toward his side, fewer offered samples;
- Yasmin hosting → provenance catalogue / envelope / second chair reserved;
- Dima settlement → sealed envelope + claim paper occupying table space;
- Wong handoff → parcel under or beside seat, packing string/paper;
- missed meeting → second glass untouched / empty chair / folded note;
- public sanction gossip → folded Octopus notice visible but not automatically readable;
- toad-circle aftermath → strange group-chat phone notification, damp container trace, odd silence.

# 5. Location identities — world-building targets

These are visual/environmental targets, not final background art prompts.

## 5.1 Workbench / player's desk

Function:
- cognitive centre, not home-decor lifestyle scene.

Visual language:
- handled paper, harbour grime, wood/metal surface, notes, receipts, phone, notebook, ledger;
- objects accumulate and overlap;
- visual order may increase as player learns the economy;
- direct view or trace of harbour outside frame if composition permits.

Reference balance:
- Death and Taxes physicality;
- Pomegranates material still-life logic;
- Sunflower's own dense documentary economy.

## 5.2 Harbour berth / Aspen

Function:
- movement, cargo, deadlines, route choice.

Visual language:
- rope, wet metal/wood, manifests, crates, weather, boat edges, layered horizon;
- cool harbour field with rust/terracotta/cargo accents;
- background crews and small boats create micro-stories;
- not a clean marina.

## 5.3 Wong's counter / storage

Function:
- cheap custody, packing, handoff, salvage, small services.

Visual language:
- dense but sorted-by-practice rather than whimsical junk;
- reused boxes, labels, string, bottle crates, chargers, locks, tags, paper;
- narrow paths and reachable shelves;
- floor-level and low anchors matter if Wong retains current Dog presentation;
- many non-utility traces but no cute junk-shop rainbow.

## 5.4 Juan's nursery

Function:
- biological productive capital + scientific curiosity + risk.

Visual language:
- humid greenhouse/yard, tables at different heights, containers, labels, roots, cuttings, tools;
- plant mass can create soupsoup-like hidden zones;
- handwritten measurement culture;
- ordinary plastic, wire, old wood and glass mixed with living colour;
- toads can plausibly hide without becoming supernatural.

## 5.5 Yasmin's viewing room / auction

Function:
- provenance, capital, access, social ranking, uncertainty.

Visual language:
- restrained Persian/Iranian chamber influence without direct historical reproduction;
- arches/screens or architectural divisions;
- deep green/wine/ink/cream with brass/silver accents;
- lots isolated on cloth/pedestal/table rather than retail shelves;
- candle/practical low light only where plausible;
- attendants/observers separated by furniture and negative space;
- Chess of the Wind pressure + Modan social bodies + Pomegranates object choreography.

## 5.6 Dima's back room

Function:
- private settlement, claims, proxy, enforcement, black-market access.

Visual language:
- small, procedural, quiet;
- envelopes, keys, tags, numbered papers, bag/box, old calculator/ledger;
- enough privacy without gangster cliché;
- a room that looks used because transactions need somewhere to become real;
- current Seagull presentation requires believable perch/desk/railing anchors.

## 5.7 Octopus Clearing

Function:
- civic institution, public commitment and settlement.

Visual language:
- standardized trays, tags, receipts, slips, owner labels, queues/slots, returned piles;
- geometric repeatable structure;
- institutional colour discipline distinct from private merchants;
- no personified Octopus mascot desk;
- client goods remain visibly client-owned until settlement;
- public tape and release/return should be visually legible.

This can borrow formal tableau logic from Pomegranates and institutional object grammar from Death and Taxes without looking bureaucratically monochrome.

## 5.8 Sonya's kitchen

Function:
- non-market household allocation and hospitality.

Visual language:
- small, worn, warm, practical;
- borrowed chairs, fish preparation, serving dishes, household plant, old cloth, ordinary storage;
- no commercial restaurant staging;
- invitation payoff should feel materially intimate, not reward-screen grandiose.

## 5.9 Cliff path

Function:
- risk, mobility, distance from commercial harbour.

Visual language:
- strong negative space;
- simplified terrain masses;
- wind, sky, sea, rock;
- tiny human/object scale against landscape;
- Onewheel and body posture matter more than UI;
- Pomegranates-like stillness and Modan-like ordinary body can coexist.

# 6. Colour direction — richer than the current generic harbour palette

Do not use one global palette.

Use recurring **material families** across location-specific palettes:

- cacao / burnt umber;
- terracotta / rust;
- marigold / old yellow;
- ultramarine / cobalt;
- turquoise / oxidised teal;
- bottle green / olive;
- dusty rose / pomegranate red;
- aubergine / muted purple;
- warm plaster / old paper;
- charcoal / ink;
- small brass/copper accents.

Use colour to organise attention, not to indicate RPG rarity.

High saturation is allowed, but it should feel matte/printed rather than LED/neon/glossy.

---

# 7. Pattern and ornament

Pattern is important but should be spatially controlled.

Good uses:

- one tiled strip;
- woven seat/textile;
- paper cut-out edge;
- screen/lattice;
- printed label family;
- auction cloth;
- plant shadows;
- crate/grid geometry;
- architectural niche repetition.

Bad use:

> every surface receives a different ornate pattern.

Dense scenes need large quiet colour fields to remain readable.

---

# 8. Micro-story density

Each WIDE should contain things worth looking at even when no route action is available.

Examples:

- someone has moved a chair to avoid someone else;
- a parcel has been rewrapped;
- a half-cleaned spill remains;
- a background visitor is counting coins;
- a gull watches food;
- a customer is asleep or reading;
- an extra glass implies someone expected;
- a note is pinned upside down;
- a plant has grown around a tag;
- an Octopus tray contains returned goods;
- a hidden toad is part of the same visual ecology.

Do not annotate these by default.

Some details may later become useful because world state changes; others remain texture forever.

---

# 9. Animation / motion restraint

Sunflower should not become a constantly wiggling mobile game.

Use small loops:

- smoke curl;
- hanging cloth edge;
- water drip;
- fan/plant movement;
- glass condensation;
- hand tapping;
- page lifting;
- distant boat motion;
- eye/head turn rarely;
- toad micro-movement only under accessibility or authored event conditions.

Stillness is part of the visual language.

Sun Moment works because ordinary motion stops.

# 10. Audio direction — deferred production, current aesthetic target

BGM implementation may remain later, but future audio should be designed toward:

- instrumental North-African / Middle-Eastern fusion;
- jazz / neo-soul / R&B harmonic and rhythmic language;
- occasional gnawa-informed pulse;
- oud, qanun, ney, guembri, riq/bendir or related timbres when appropriate;
- Rhodes/electric piano, upright/electric bass, brushed drums, restrained sax/trumpet/guitar where useful;
- warm, groove-based, nocturnal, urban and cosmopolitan rather than cinematic "desert" scoring.

Avoid:

- generic belly-dance loops;
- EDM "Arabian" presets;
- constant dramatic oud tremolo;
- faux-mystical desert flute;
- tourist-restaurant soundtrack;
- vocal tracks that compete with dialogue as default ambience.

Joel's Bar can have the richest fusion palette; other locations may rely more on environmental sound.

When music production begins:

- source only original or appropriately licensed tracks;
- maintain a repository licence ledger;
- prefer CC0 / CC BY / explicit commercial-use licences or commissioned/original music;
- do not treat "free to stream" as free to redistribute.

No track selection is frozen by this file.
